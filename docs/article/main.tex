% ABSTRACT
% =========================
\begin{abstract}
Solar photovoltaic generation is expanding rapidly across tropical Latin
America: Colombia alone has surpassed 1~GW of installed capacity and targets
9~GW by 2030 \citep{upme2023}. Reliable short-term forecasts of global
horizontal irradiance (GHI) are essential for risk-aware reserve scheduling,
yet a point forecast alone cannot signal whether it was produced under
conditions the model has genuinely learned from or under atypical,
data-scarce ones. Forecasting in the tropics is further complicated by
frequent convective cloud systems that can cut surface irradiance by more
than 80\% within minutes and are poorly captured by coarse-resolution
numerical weather prediction. This paper proposes a graph neural network
framework for short-term GHI forecasting from GOES-16 geostationary imagery,
coupled with an \textbf{epistemic uncertainty-quantification layer} based on
Stochastic Gradient Langevin Dynamics (SGLD) posterior weight sampling,
intended to flag forecasts made under data-scarce conditions. We introduce
\textbf{GraphSAGE-LSTM}, which treats each satellite patch as a graph of
spatially connected pixels, aggregates neighbourhoods via GraphSAGE, and
passes the resulting embedding sequence through an LSTM, comparing a fixed
unweighted nearest-neighbour graph against a tuned, distance-weighted
variant. The model is evaluated against ResNet-LSTM, FlatMLP, SARIMA, and
persistence baselines at two climatically contrasting Colombian sites ---
El~Paso (C{\'e}sar), a high-irradiance lowland site, and Universidad de los
Andes (Bogot{\'a}), an equatorial mountain station --- across 1-, 3-, and
6-hour horizons. Four-seed comparisons show graph-based encoders are
competitive with or superior to convolutional baselines at multi-hour
horizons, while absolute skill is markedly lower at the mountain site,
indicating that model selection should be climate-aware. There, a
spatial-ablation baseline discarding all within-patch structure proves
surprisingly competitive, quantifying how little of the achievable skill is
attributable to spatial encoding. The weighted-graph ablation and the
uncertainty decomposition remain in progress: the epistemic estimator is not
yet finalised, as a fixed-step SGLD chain drifts over the full sampling
budget on these over-parameterised networks. This work contributes a
reproducible GOES-16 benchmark for the underrepresented tropical Andean
climate and a posterior-sampling approach to epistemic uncertainty
quantification for satellite-based solar forecasting.
\end{abstract}

% HIGHLIGHTS
% =========================
\begin{highlights}
\item GraphSAGE-LSTM for GHI forecasting, with a fixed vs.\ tuned $k$-NN
      graph ablation.
\item Compared against ResNet-LSTM, FlatMLP, SARIMA, and persistence at two
      Colombian sites.
\item Spatial ablation shows limited marginal value of satellite spatial
      features at the mountain site.
\item A posterior-sampling layer designed to flag data-scarce forecast
      conditions; estimator not yet finalised.
\item A reproducible GOES-16 benchmark for underrepresented tropical Andean
      climates.
\end{highlights}
